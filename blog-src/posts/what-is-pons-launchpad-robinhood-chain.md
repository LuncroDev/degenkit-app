If you search for Robinhood Chain tokens, one name keeps coming up: **Pons**, also written as Pons Launcher or PonsLauncher. It is a token launchpad, often called "pump.fun for Robinhood Chain", and it became one of the chain's busiest apps within weeks of launch. This guide explains what Pons is, how it works, the risks, and how it differs from creating your own token.

## What is Pons?

Pons is a **non-custodial token launchpad** built for Robinhood Chain. Anyone can create a fixed-supply token from their own wallet, with no code, and the token can be traded straight away. Public coverage describes it as a launcher for tokens with a **fixed supply of 1 billion**, placed directly into a trading pool ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/resources/what-robinhood-chain-ethereum-layer-2-network-tokenized-stocks?amp=1)).

Robinhood Chain itself is an Ethereum Layer 2 built on Arbitrum, with ETH as the gas token. Its public mainnet opened on July 1, 2026, and Pons went live there in mid-July ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/pons-token-robinhood-chain-pump-fun-rival), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/markets/pons/)).

## Pons, Pons Launcher or Pons Launchpad?

They are the same app. The product calls itself Pons, but people search for it as **Pons Launcher**, **PonsLauncher** (one word) or **Pons launchpad**, and some guides use those names too ([Smithii](https://smithii.io/en/launch-token-pons-launcher-robinhood-chain/)). DefiLlama lists it simply as Pons and describes a launchpad for fixed-supply tokens that move toward a liquidity "graduation" on Robinhood Chain ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/pons)).

One caution: because the name is popular, look-alike sites exist. Reach the app only through links you trust, and check the address bar before you connect a wallet.

## What is a Pons launcher token?

The phrase is used in two ways:

- **A token launched through Pons.** Any coin created on the launchpad is a "Pons launcher token" in everyday use. There are a very large number of them, and most are short-lived.
- **PONS itself**, the token that shares the launchpad's name. The next section covers it.

When you see the phrase, check which one the writer means before you act on it.

## What is PONS, the token?

**PONS** is the token associated with the Pons launchpad. It is not issued by Robinhood Markets, and it is not Robinhood stock or a tokenized version of it ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/pons-token-robinhood-chain-pump-fun-rival)). Reports describe a model where a share of the launchpad's fees funds buybacks of PONS ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/it/crypto/pons-explained)). Sources do not fully agree on how PONS and the launchpad are connected, so read the project's own documents before you rely on any claim. Treat PONS like any other volatile token: its price can move sharply in both directions.

## How the Pons launchpad works

1. You connect a wallet on Robinhood Chain.
2. You enter a name, symbol and image.
3. You pay a launch fee and sign one transaction. One source reports a flat fee of about 0.0005 ETH ([AirdropAlert](https://airdropalert.com/?p=324531)), but confirm the current fee on the site.
4. The token is created with a fixed supply and starts trading.

Several sources also say launched tokens' liquidity is locked by the protocol. That protects against one kind of rug pull, but it does **not** tell you whether a token is a good project.

## Risks to know before you buy a Pons token

- **Anyone can launch.** Permissionless means most launches are copies, jokes or scams. Check the contract and the deployer before you buy.
- **Locked liquidity is not a guarantee.** Holders can still lose everything when a token's price goes to zero.
- **Competition changes things fast.** In early August, Uniswap launched its own launchpad with zero fees and took a large share of launch volume ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/377349/pons-robinhood-chain?amp=1)). Launchpad rankings shift weekly.
- **Scam reports exist.** Crypto Briefing has linked a batch of Robinhood Chain launches to an alleged rug-pull group ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/markets/pons/)).

Never buy only because a token trades on a popular launchpad.

## How to check a Pons launcher token before you buy

1. **Open the token in the explorer.** Confirm the contract address matches the one on the launchpad page, and look at when it was created.
2. **Look at the holders.** If a few wallets hold most of the supply, they can move the price on their own.
3. **Check the liquidity.** A thin pool means a small sell can drop the price sharply.
4. **Try a small amount first.** Buy and sell a tiny position to confirm the token can actually be sold.

## FAQ

### Is Pons Launcher the same as Pons launchpad?

Yes. Pons Launcher, PonsLauncher and Pons launchpad are different names people use for the same token launchpad on Robinhood Chain.

### Is Pons made by Robinhood?

No. Pons is a separate project that runs on Robinhood Chain. It is not issued, owned or endorsed by Robinhood Markets, and neither is DegenKit.

### Is a Pons launcher token safe to buy?

Not by default. Anyone can launch a token, so each one needs its own check of the contract, the holders and the liquidity. Locked liquidity reduces one risk but does not make a token valuable.

### Can I create a Robinhood Chain token without Pons?

Yes. A token generator such as the DegenKit Robinhood Chain token creator deploys a fixed-supply or tax token straight to your wallet, and you add liquidity yourself.

### What does it cost to launch on Pons?

Fees can change, so check the Pons site for the current launch fee. You also pay Robinhood Chain gas in ETH.
