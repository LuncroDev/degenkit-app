Why Crypto Crashed in Early November 2026: A Hack, the Fed and an AI Sell-Off

The record Binance hack, a hawkish Fed, record liquidations and a tech-stock rout hit crypto in the same ten days. Here is what happened, in order, and what the market looks like now.

DegenKitNovember 7, 2026 · 5 min read

Between October 28 and November 6, 2026, crypto lost a large part of its value in a series of shocks. There was no single cause. A Fed decision, the largest theft in crypto history, US midterm elections and a tech-stock sell-off came one after another, and leveraged traders made every move bigger.

By November 7, the total crypto market was worth about $2.58 trillion, according to The Currency Analytics. The Fear & Greed Index stood at 25, "Extreme Fear".

Figures as of November 7, 2026

Prices move every minute. The numbers here come from the sources linked in each section, and this is news, not financial advice.

The timeline

October 28 — the Fed holds, but two members want a hike. The Federal Reserve kept rates at 3.75%–4.00% in a 10–2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voted for a quarter-point increase, and Chair Warsh called inflation "too high" (Wolf Street). Markets had hoped for the opposite.

October 29 — Binance is hacked. Attackers drained hot wallets on 11 blockchains in about three hours. Binance estimates the loss at about $6.3 billion, and Elliptic puts it at $6.43 billion, the largest theft in history (Elliptic). Binance paused withdrawals. Bitcoin fell below $79,000, and about $628 million of leveraged positions were liquidated within an hour (CryptoTicker). We cover the hack in detail in the Binance hack explained.

October 31 – November 1. The FBI attributed the theft to North Korea's TraderTraitor group. Binance said it would cover all losses and began reopening withdrawals.

November 3 — midterm bounce. Bitcoin briefly climbed back above $80,000 after the US midterm elections, then gave it all back.

November 5 — the AI sell-off. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% and the S&P 500 fell 4.3% as investors questioned spending on AI infrastructure (crypto.news). Crypto followed: Bitcoin fell 5.3% to about $76,000, Ether 7.2% to about $2,250, and Solana 6.5% to about $89 (CryptoTicker).

November 6 — Asia joins in. South Korea's KOSPI closed 6% lower after a brief trading halt, and Japan's Nikkei fell almost 6%. The same day, Ethereum developers delayed the next upgrade's test-network activation (KuCoin News).

Why it fell so hard

Every one of these events would have caused a dip on its own. The crash came from three amplifiers:

  1. Leverage. About $1.26 billion of positions were liquidated in the 24 hours around November 5, roughly $1.12 billion of them longs. Forced selling pushes prices lower, which triggers more liquidations. See how liquidations turn a dip into a crash.
  2. ETF outflows. US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $430.2 million on November 5 alone, and Ether ETFs nearly $100 million.
  3. Correlation with tech stocks. When AI and chip stocks sold off, crypto was treated as one more risky asset and sold with them.

Where the big coins stand

  • Bitcoin is holding near $76,000, roughly 40% below its October 2025 high. Read Bitcoin after the crash.
  • Ethereum is around $2,240, with support near $2,200. Read where Ethereum is heading.
  • Solana is sitting right on its 200-day average near $89. Read Solana at its 200-day line.

What to watch next week

  • Whether ETF flows turn positive again.
  • Whether Bitcoin holds the $75,000 area.
  • Any new statement from the Fed on inflation.
  • How much of the stolen Binance funds are frozen or moved.

What it means if you are building

Crashes don't stop launches, but they change the rules: less attention, thinner liquidity and buyers who are more cautious. Our playbook on launching a token during a crash covers what to do differently.
