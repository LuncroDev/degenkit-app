How to plan token allocation

Tokenomics is the plan for who holds the supply and why. Buyers read it to judge risk: a large share in a few team wallets means those wallets can move the price, while a large share in the liquidity pool means the price is harder to push around.

A simple, common split for a small community token puts most of the supply into liquidity, keeps a modest share for the community and marketing, and gives the team a small share that vests over time. There is no rule that fits every project, so use the chart to check that your split matches what you plan to tell holders.

With a DegenKit ERC-20 the whole supply is minted to your wallet at creation and no more can ever be minted. You then move each share to its wallet or pool. Read the token launch checklist and work out your pool numbers with the liquidity calculator.

Questions people ask

What is tokenomics?

Tokenomics describes a token's supply and how it is distributed: how many tokens exist, who receives them, and whether any are locked or vested.

What is a good token allocation?

There is no universal answer. Buyers generally prefer a large share in liquidity and a small, vested team share. Whatever you choose, publish it and stick to it.

How many tokens should I create?

Supply is a presentation choice. One billion is common for community tokens because it gives a low unit price. Supply alone does not change the market cap.

Can I change the supply later?

Not with a DegenKit token. The supply is fixed at creation and the contract has no mint function.

Does this tool create a token?

No. It is a planner. To create the token, use the token creator on the network you want.
